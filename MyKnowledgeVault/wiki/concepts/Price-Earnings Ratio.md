# Price-Earnings Ratio

From [[Investment Guide Notes]] (page 2). Supports rule 2 of the
[[Investment Entry Checklist]].

- **TTM P/E** = price ÷ earnings from the last 12 months → *reality*: what the
  company has already delivered.
- **Forward P/E** = price ÷ expected earnings for the next 12 months →
  *expectations*: what the market expects it to deliver.

## Three cases

| Case | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Forward < TTM (best) | Earnings expected to grow; valuation improving; P/E compresses so price can rise without overvaluation | Healthy growth setup; good for holding or buying pullbacks |
| Forward ≈ TTM | Earnings expected flat; price already reflects reality | OK for stable businesses; don't expect big upside |
| Forward > TTM (warning) | Earnings expected to decline, or forecasts were cut; P/E expanding leaves price vulnerable | Risk of valuation shock; avoid or reduce position |

## Context matters

- **Growth companies**: a high TTM P/E is OK *if* Forward P/E drops sharply.
- **Mature companies**: TTM P/E should be low/moderate; Forward P/E similar or
  slightly lower.

## When P/E is not useful

Loss-making companies, cyclical or distorted earnings, or heavy one-off items —
use revenue growth, cash flow, or margins instead.
